=================================
Theory Overview
=================================

This chapter is the entry point to the mathematics behind Irene. It states the
objects each relaxation family builds, in the order the package constructs them,
and points to the full treatment on the corresponding chapter page. Nothing here
is implementation detail: for API-level material start at :doc:`introduction`,
and for symbol conventions see the :ref:`notation-index` in :doc:`appendix`.

The underlying manuscript is cited as *the companion paper* below; its Section 2
contains the same chain of constructions in archival form.

------------------------------
The optimization problem
------------------------------

Let :math:`X` be a nonempty topological space and :math:`A` a unital
sub-algebra of continuous functions on :math:`X` separating points of
:math:`X`. Irene addresses

.. math::
   :label: theory-pop

   \begin{aligned}
     \min \quad & f(x) \\
     \textrm{subject to} \quad & g_i(x) \ge 0, \qquad i = 1,\dots,m,
   \end{aligned}

with feasibility set :math:`K = \{x \in X : g_i(x) \ge 0,\; i=1,\dots,m\}` and
optimum value :math:`\rho`. Writing :math:`\mathcal{M}_1^+(K)` for the
probability Borel measures supported on :math:`K`, the value coincides with the
moment formulation

.. math::
   :label: theory-rho

   \rho = \inf_{\mu \in \mathcal{M}_1^+(K)} \int f \, d\mu
        = \inf_{\substack{L(Q) \ge 0 \\ L(1) = 1}} L(f),

where :math:`Q_{\mathbf{g}}` is the quadratic module generated by the
constraints, :math:`\sigma_0 + \sigma_1 g_1 + \dots + \sigma_m g_m` with each
:math:`\sigma_i` a sum of squares in :math:`A`. Equality of the three quantities
in :eq:`theory-rho` is what turns an optimization problem into a
*truncated moment problem*, and it requires the Archimedean hypothesis
:math:`M \pm h \in Q` for all :math:`h \in A` and some :math:`M > 0`; see
:doc:`optim` for the full derivation, including the Marshall/Haviland and
Jacobi representation steps.

The decisive point for the rest of the package: Irene does **not** assume
:math:`A = \mathbb{R}[x_1,\dots,x_n]`. Any unital commutative algebra works, and
:math:`K` only has to be bounded. That is the generalization the whole design
rests on.

------------------------------
Step 1 -- the algebraic layer
------------------------------

Problems are represented over the semigroup algebra :math:`\mathbb{R}[S]` of a
finitely generated commutative semigroup :math:`S`, so that

.. math::
   :label: theory-algebra

   f = \sum_{\alpha \in \mathrm{supp}(f)} c_\alpha \, \alpha,
   \qquad c_\alpha \in \mathbb{R},

with :math:`\alpha` a semigroup element rather than a monomial in fixed
generators. Quotient structures are handled by reduction in the semigroup
representation. This buys two things at once: exponent vectors are available
directly for convex geometry, and the same representation feeds the SDP, GP and
SONC families. Registered derivations extend the layer with Leibniz-rule maps
:math:`D : \mathbb{R}[S] \to \mathbb{R}[S]`.

See :doc:`algebra` for the group-ring construction and :doc:`program` for
``OptimizationProblem``, the object that carries :math:`f`, the :math:`g_i` and
the even-degree convention used to build relaxations.

------------------------------
Step 2 -- moment/SOS hierarchy
------------------------------

At relaxation order :math:`t`, introduce moment variables :math:`y_\alpha` on
:math:`\Lambda_t = \{\alpha : |\alpha| \le t\}` and the moment and localizing
matrices

.. math::
   :label: theory-momentmatrices

   M_t(y)_{\alpha,\beta} = y_{\alpha+\beta},
   \qquad
   M_t(g_i y)_{\alpha,\beta} = \sum_\gamma h_{i,\gamma}\, y_{\alpha+\beta+\gamma},

for :math:`g_i = \sum_\gamma h_{i,\gamma} x^\gamma` and
:math:`\alpha,\beta \in B_t`. The order-:math:`t` SDP is

.. math::
   :label: theory-sdp

   \min \; y_f = \sum_\alpha f_\alpha y_\alpha
   \quad \textrm{s.t.} \quad
   M_t(y) \succeq 0, \;\; M_t(g_i y) \succeq 0 \;\; (i = 1,\dots,m).

Its optimal values form a monotonically non-decreasing sequence

.. math::
   :label: theory-chain

   \gamma_t \le \gamma_{t+1} \le \cdots \le \rho,

with :math:`\gamma_t \to \rho` as :math:`t \to \infty`, and finite termination
at some :math:`t^*` under the Archimedean condition (Putinar). Each
:math:`\gamma_t` is a certified lower bound together with an SOS certificate
:math:`f - \gamma_t \in Q_{\mathbf{g}}`.

See :doc:`sdp` for the SDP formulation and solver routing, and :doc:`optim` for
the functional-analytic derivation.

--------------------------------
Step 3 -- structure exploitation
--------------------------------

The hierarchy above is correct but expensive. Three reduction families shrink
the semidefinite program without weakening the certificate:

- **Correlative sparsity** (:doc:`sparsity`) -- build the sparsity graph on the
  variables and use its connected components to replace one large moment matrix
  by a block-diagonal family.
- **Newton polytope pruning** (:doc:`newton_polytope`) -- use
  :math:`\mathrm{New}(f) = \mathrm{conv}\{\alpha : c_\alpha \neq 0\}` and Minkowski
  sums over the system to filter candidate monomials. Note that hull membership
  alone does not certify removability, so the pipeline currently retains the full
  degree-bounded support by design.
- **Border bases** (:doc:`border_basis`) -- compute a basis of
  :math:`V_d / (I \cap V_d)` for :math:`V_d = \mathrm{span}\{x^\alpha :
  |\alpha| \le d\}` without fixing a monomial ordering, avoiding the numerical
  conditioning problems of Gröbner bases.

Plus :doc:`sparse_moment`, the toric extension of the moment method that
computes real points directly on the semigroup ring :math:`R[A]`.

----------------------------------
Step 4 -- GP and SONC alternatives
----------------------------------

Not every problem is best attacked through moment matrices. Two
certificate families reduce to *geometric programming*, which is polynomial-time
per iteration and insensitive to degree growth.

**Geometric programming** (:doc:`geometric`). With :math:`g_0 = -f` and a
transform matrix :math:`H`, build :math:`h_j = \sum_k H_{k,j} g_k` and minimize

.. math::
   :label: theory-gp

   p(\mu, w, z)
   = \sum_{j=1}^{m} h_+(h_j)\mu_j
   + \sum_{\alpha \in \Delta^{<d}} (d-\deg\alpha)
     \left(\frac{w_\alpha}{d}\right)^{\frac{d}{d-\deg\alpha}}
     \prod_i \left(\frac{\alpha_i}{z_{\alpha,i}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha_i}{d-\deg\alpha}}

with :math:`d` the effective even relaxation order. The matrix :math:`H` is
structural, not merely numerical: it decides which combinations of objective and
constraints the GP model sees, and hence the sign and support pattern of the
resulting inequalities.

**SONC** (:doc:`sonc`). With :math:`G(\mu) = f - \sum_i \mu_i g_i`, the
constrained circuit structure is encoded by four families over circuit weights
:math:`a_{\beta,j}`, auxiliary bounds :math:`b_\beta` and barycentric weights
:math:`\lambda^{(\beta)}` satisfying :math:`\sum_j \lambda_j^{(\beta)} = 1`:

.. math::
   :label: theory-sonc

   \sum_j a_{\beta,j} \le G(\mu)_{\alpha(j)},
   \qquad
   \prod_j \left(\frac{a_{\beta,j}}{\lambda_j^{(\beta)}}\right)^{\lambda_j^{(\beta)}} \ge b_\beta,
   \qquad
   G(\mu)_\beta^{+} \le b_\beta,
   \qquad
   G(\mu)_\beta^{-} \le b_\beta .

The GP objective is minimized over :math:`(\mu, a, b)`, branching on whether
:math:`\lambda_0^{(\beta)} > 0`.

--------------------------------
Step 5 -- combining certificates
--------------------------------

Let :math:`\Sigma_{n,2d}` and :math:`C_{n,2d}` denote the SOS and SONC cones of
degree :math:`2d` in :math:`n` variables. Neither contains the other in general,
so their Minkowski sum is a strictly larger certificate family:

.. math::
   :label: theory-sosonc

   (\Sigma + C)_{n,2d} = \{s + c \mid s \in \Sigma_{n,2d},\; c \in C_{n,2d}\},
   \qquad
   f_{\Sigma+C}^{*} = \sup\{\lambda \mid f - \lambda \in (\Sigma+C)_{n,2d}\}.

Because each cone embeds in the sum,

.. math::
   :label: theory-sandwich

   \max\{f_\Sigma^{*},\, f_C^{*}\} \;\le\; f_{\Sigma+C}^{*} \;\le\; f^{*},

and the bound is never worse than either pure approach. The two-step
preprocessing of :doc:`sosonc` achieves it with one SDP plus one signomial
program: SOS-first finds :math:`g^* \in \Sigma_{n,2d}` minimizing a convex
distance to :math:`f`, then a SONC relaxation is solved on the residual
:math:`h = f - g^*`, yielding :math:`f_{\Sigma+C}^* \ge \mu^*` with the explicit
decomposition :math:`f - \mu^* = g^* + (h - \mu^*) \in \Sigma + C`. SONC-first
swaps the roles.

--------------------------------------
Step 6 -- leaving the polynomial world
--------------------------------------

Two extensions push past polynomials in finitely many variables:

- **Differential SDP** (:doc:`dsdp_mean`, built on the derivation machinery of
  :doc:`algebra`). A family of commuting derivations :math:`D_1,\dots,D_m`
  defines an operator semigroup :math:`\Theta = \langle \delta_1,\dots,\delta_m \rangle`
  and a jet space :math:`\Theta Y = \{\theta y_j\}` satisfying the multi-index
  Leibniz rule

  .. math::
     :label: theory-leibniz

     D_i\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r} \theta_k y_{j_k}\right)
     = \sum_{k=1}^{r} (\delta_i \theta_k y_{j_k}) \prod_{l \neq k} \theta_l y_{j_l}.

  Applying the :math:`D_i` to algebraic differential relations
  :math:`\mathcal{F}` constructs the truncated differential ideal
  :math:`\mathcal{I}_{\le 2d} = [\mathcal{F}]_{\le 2d}`, and the quotient
  :math:`\mathbb{R}[S]/\mathcal{I}_{\le 2d}` supplies the monomial basis of the
  moment matrix. Weighted power mean forms
  :math:`M_{q,p}(X,w) = \sum_i w_i X_i^p (\sum_j w_j X_j^q)^{(p-q)/q}` are used
  as certificates in the mean relaxation, generalizing both SOS and SONC.

- **Non-polynomial optimization** (:doc:`nonpopsdp`, :doc:`approx`). When
  separating functions from transcendental terms is impossible -- :math:`x`,
  :math:`\sin x` and :math:`e^x` are not algebraically independent, and the
  dependence admits no finite relation set -- Irene replaces the transcendental
  terms by polynomial surrogates (Taylor or Chebyshev, with the
  radius-of-convergence pitfall in mind), solves the resulting polynomial
  optimization problem, and adds a redundant ball constraint per
  Josz--Henrion (2014) to guarantee strong duality. The result is a reliable
  estimate rather than a certificate.

Further reading
------------------------------

Primary references are collected at the end of :doc:`sdp` (Lasserre 2001,
Parrilo 2000, Laurent 2009); the border-basis and SONC chapters cite their own
sources, and :doc:`dsdp_mean` documents the CGIK convergence framework for the
truncated moment problem on unital commutative algebras. For the package-level
narrative -- layer structure, solver routing, benchmark evidence and limitations
-- see the companion paper's Sections 3 to 7.
